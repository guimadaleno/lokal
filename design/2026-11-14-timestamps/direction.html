Message times

Scrolling back, see when you asked and when the model answered. Three places the time could sit, compared in place below. Recommended: B, the time at the two edges of each turn.

Principles

In the order they win.

  1. The conversation leads.A time is looked up, never read in passing: it recedes behind every word of the turn.
  2. A time sits beside what it dates.The eye shouldn't have to hunt a turn for when it began.
  3. The left edge stays Lokal's.> and $ start each block at column one, where the eye starts reading; times go right.
  4. On screen only.The model, the saved conversation and the other sessions get nothing of it.
  5. One way to write it.The computer's local time, 24-hour; the date only where a day changes.
  6. The words win the room.A line without room for its time keeps its text and loses the time.

The time itself

The same in all three directions.

How it's written

14:32
Hours and minutes, local, 24-hour. Seconds would be noise next to a turn.
Nov 15 09:12
The first time of a new day, and the first after a resumed session.
14:31 → 14:32
A's span, when the answer came in another minute; one time when it didn't.

Its color

Gray, not faint: faint is for what goes away (the status) and for lines that only separate. A time is read when it's looked for, and faint on a dark terminal is too dim to read at a glance. Flip Time in above to judge.

How it arrives (B and C)

While the request is typed, the last turn's status sits at the right
Enter: the status goes with the hint, and the time is written in its place

Nothing moves. A terminal's scrollback is fixed, so each time is written once, as its line is done, and stays.

Everywhere else

What's dated beyond requests and answers, in all three.

  • Mail from another session carries its time at the right of its · api-1: line, as a request does: it's a message too.
  • Your ! commands and @name mail are lines you typed at the >, and are dated like a request (B, C; A dates the turn they start).
  • A message queued while Lokal works shows as > … mid-turn; in B and C it carries the time Enter queued it.
  • A wrapped request carries its time on its last row, where the status would have sat; a last row without room goes without.
  • Times already printed stay when the window is resized, like the rule: the terminal owns those lines.
  • A piped answer and a one-shot request get none: they're for scripts.

Decisions

What's yours, and what this direction took on its own.

Yours

  • A, B or C. B is recommended: each time sits by what it dates, in the column the status already uses, and two times a turn stay quiet. A puts when you asked under the answer, away from the request. C dates every step, useful for long runs in auto, but most steps share their turn's minute, so its column mostly repeats itself.
  • Gray or faint. Gray is recommended, for the reason above.

Taken here, open to change

  • 24-hour, no seconds, read from the computer's clock, as the system prompt's date already is.
  • No setting to turn it off. At this weight it costs nothing to whoever ignores it; one can come if someone asks.
  • Nothing for the model. You asked for something to see; the model already knows the date, and a time on every message would cost tokens each request.
